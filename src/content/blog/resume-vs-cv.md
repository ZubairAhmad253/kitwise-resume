"Please send your CV." "Attach your resume." Depending on where you apply, these can mean exactly the same thing or two quite different documents.

## The short answer

- In the **US and Canada**, a *resume* is a short summary of your career (one or two pages), and a *CV* is a long academic record used for research and university jobs.
- In the **UK, Ireland, most of Europe, the Middle East, India and Africa**, *CV* is simply the everyday word for what Americans call a resume.

So if a company in Doha, Dubai or London asks for your CV, they almost always mean a two-page document, not a twelve-page academic record.

## The US resume

- One page for early-career roles, two pages for experienced ones.
- No photo, date of birth or marital status: US employers avoid these to prevent discrimination claims.
- Focused on the job you want: older or unrelated roles are shortened or left out.
- Usually printed on US Letter paper (8.5 × 11 inches).

## The academic CV

- As long as it needs to be: five, ten or more pages is normal for a senior researcher.
- Complete rather than selective: every appointment, publication, grant, talk, course taught and student supervised.
- Sections ordered by what the committee cares about, often education and appointments first, then publications.

Templates like **Traditional CV** and **Scholar Block** are built for this kind of document, with numbered sections and publication lists that break cleanly across pages.

## The international CV

Outside North America, "CV" usually means a document much like a US resume, with some local habits:

- **Length:** two pages is common; three is fine for long careers.
- **Paper:** A4.
- **Photo:** common in the Gulf and parts of Europe and Asia, rarely used in the UK and Ireland. When you include one, use a simple, professional head-and-shoulders photo.
- **Personal details:** in the Gulf, some employers expect nationality, visa status or notice period. Add them only when the ad or recruiter asks, and never your passport number.

## How to tell what they want

Read the job ad and the country it's in:

- A university or research institute asking for a "CV" wants the academic version.
- A company in the UK, Europe, the Gulf or Asia asking for a "CV" wants a two-page career summary.
- A US company asking for a "resume" wants one or two pages, without a photo.

When in doubt, send a focused two-page version. It's easier for a recruiter to ask for more than to read through too much.
