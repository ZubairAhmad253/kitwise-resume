Most medium and large employers run every application through an **applicant tracking system (ATS)**. It pulls your resume into a database, splits it into fields (name, jobs, dates, skills) and lets recruiters search and filter. If the system misreads your file, a strong candidate can look like a weak match.

The good news: what helps an ATS also helps the human who reads your resume next.

## Use real text, not pictures of text

An ATS reads the text layer of your file. A resume saved as an image, or a PDF made by scanning a printout, has no text to read. Export your resume as a PDF from the tool you wrote it in, or send a Word file if the job ad asks for one.

## Keep headings standard

The system looks for sections by name. "Experience", "Work experience", "Education", "Skills" and "Certifications" are recognised almost everywhere. Creative names like "My journey" or "What I bring" may not be.

You can still give your resume personality through design, colour and wording, just not through the section names.

## Put the job title and employer on every role

For each job, include:

- your job title
- the employer's name
- the city and country
- start and end dates, as month and year

Missing dates are one of the most common reasons an ATS mis-counts your years of experience.

## Use the words from the job ad

Recruiters search the database with the words in their own ad: "IFRS", "Kubernetes", "patient safety", "forklift licence". If you have the skill, use the same word for it. Put the most important ones in your skills list and again in the bullet point where you used them.

Paste the job ad into **Check → Match a job ad** in the builder to see which of its key words your resume already uses and which are missing. Only add the ones that are true for you.

## Avoid layouts that confuse parsers

Most modern systems handle two columns well, but a few older ones still read across columns line by line. If a job site's preview of your resume looks scrambled, switch to a one-column template. Your content moves with you, so it only takes a click.

Other things to avoid:

- text inside images, charts or icons that carry meaning
- contact details only in the page header or footer
- tables used to lay out the whole page

## Write bullets that rank and read well

An ATS finds the keywords; a person decides. Start each bullet with what you did and end with the result: "Cut month-end close from 12 to 5 days by automating reconciliations." Numbers make a bullet specific and easy to trust.

## Check it before you send it

Run **Check** in the builder before you download. It flags missing dates, weak bullets, placeholders you forgot to fill in and non-standard headings, and gives you a score out of 100. None of it leaves your device.
