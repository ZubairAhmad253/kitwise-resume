"Responsible for customer service" tells a recruiter what your job was. "Handled 60 calls a day with a 96% satisfaction score" tells them how well you did it. The second one gets the interview.

You don't need a sales target to have numbers. Almost every job produces them.

## Six kinds of numbers you already have

1. **Volume:** how much you handled. Patients per shift, orders per day, sites visited, tickets closed.
2. **Size:** how big the thing was. Team size, budget, floor area, fleet, number of users.
3. **Time:** how fast or how long. Days saved, hours cut, deadlines met, years without an incident.
4. **Money:** revenue won, costs cut, savings found.
5. **Quality:** error rates, satisfaction scores, pass rates, audit results.
6. **Rank:** first place, top 5%, promoted ahead of schedule.

## Before and after

**Software engineer**
- Before: Worked on the payments API.
- After: Rebuilt the payments API in Go, cutting response time from 420 to 95 ms.

**Nurse**
- Before: Looked after patients in the emergency department.
- After: Cared for 12 to 15 patients a shift in a 60-bed emergency department.

**Driver**
- Before: Delivered goods to customers.
- After: Delivered to 30+ construction sites a week with a 99.4% on-time rate and no accidents in 480,000 km.

**Administrator**
- Before: Managed the office calendar.
- After: Scheduled meetings and travel for 6 directors across 3 time zones.

## When you don't know the exact figure

Estimate honestly, and say so with words like "about", "over" or "up to". "Trained over 20 new starters" is fine if you trained 23. Don't invent precision you don't have, and be ready to explain any number in an interview.

## A simple formula

> **Action verb + what you did + result with a number**

"Automated the weekly stock report, saving the team 5 hours a week."

In the builder, **Ideas** on each description gives you bullet starters like this for your field, with the numbers left as [placeholders] to fill in. **Check** then tells you how many of your bullets have a number in them, and reminds you about any placeholder you forgot.
